Generated by `make eval-robust`. This is a **second dataset** with a different
seed, contacts written in varied formats, and deliberately perturbed cases the
detectors were never built or tuned against: cluster sizes from 6 to 40 students
on 3-6 contacts (including one split across two institutions), names with typos,
reorderings and initials, dual degrees in other programme combinations,
admissions one day past the cutoff, and an intake breach of a single seat.

## Why each miss happened

### Ghost-student clusters

Precision 1.000, recall 0.571 (3 missed, 0 unlabelled).

- **6 students on 4 contacts (ratio 1.5) and 9 students on 5 contacts (ratio 1.8) were missed.** The rule needs at least 8 students AND at least 2.0 students per shared contact. The 6-student group fails both tests and is genuinely indistinguishable from an extended family by contact data alone. The 9-student group fails the ratio test by 0.2, which is the honest cost of a threshold that keeps 4-7 student families out of the queue.
- **A cross-institution cluster counts as one miss for a measurement reason, not a detection failure.** The 16-student cluster spanning two institutions was found, but the GHOST_CLUSTER flag carries a single institution_id, so it only matches the ground-truth key for one of the two. An officer filtering flags by the other institution would not see it. The fix is in flag attribution (raise or index the flag for every institution involved), not in the detector; it is left as a known gap rather than changed while this set is being scored.

### Near-consecutive APAAR ID runs

Precision 1.000, recall 0.500 (1 missed, 0 unlabelled).

- **A run of 16 IDs with gaps of up to 4 was missed.** The detector continues a run only while consecutive gaps are 3 or less, so a fabricator who leaves gaps of 4 breaks the run into fragments below the 10-ID minimum. This is a real evasion path. A density measure over a sliding window (how many of an institution's IDs fall inside a narrow numeric band) would not have this cliff.

### Records filed in a last-minute burst

Precision 0.667, recall 1.000 (0 missed, 1 unlabelled).

- **One unlabelled flag at 100% share.** A small institution whose single upload happened to land inside the 6-hour window trips the rule, because there is no minimum record count. On real data this would be a steady source of false positives for small colleges; the rule needs a floor on records, like the supernumerary signal already has.

### Phantom year-to-year progression

Precision 0.844, recall 1.000 (0 missed, 5 unlabelled).

- **Five unlabelled flags, all correct by the rule.** They are students whom the perturbed dual-degree scenario enrolled directly into year 2 of an online programme with no year-1 record. The rule flags exactly that shape, so these are true positives with no injected label — but they also show the real-world blind spot: a legitimate transfer or direct second-year entry looks identical to a phantom. Production would need a transfer/direct-entry marker in the upload template before this rule could be trusted at row level.

### One person with two APAAR IDs

Precision 1.000, recall 0.500 (6 missed, 0 unlabelled).

- **The biggest gap in this run: 6 of 12 records missed.** Identity matching uses an exact HMAC of the normalised name, so it survives case and whitespace changes (both caught, 2/2 each) but fails on a doubled letter, a reordered name and an initial-only surname (0/2 each). The registry client already uses rapidfuzz at a 90% threshold; identity matching does not. The fix is to block candidates on DOB + guardian phone and then compare decrypted names with rapidfuzz inside the service, which keeps the privacy property intact.

## What these numbers do and do not show

**Read this before putting the table on a slide.** The generator and the detectors
were written by the same team against the same definitions, so the dataset contains
exactly the patterns the detectors look for. High scores here mean the detectors
work as specified and have not regressed — they are *not* evidence of accuracy on
real institutional data, where patterns are messier and labels do not exist.

What the table genuinely demonstrates:

- the rules are implemented correctly and are reproducible from a fixed seed;
- the hard negative controls hold: a legal dual degree is never called fraud, a
  6-student family is never called a ghost cluster, and the clean model institution
  attracts no flags at all;
- the subtler injected cases are caught, not just the blatant ones (the 8-student
  cluster as well as the 33-student one, the 3-seat intake breach as well as the
  25-seat one);
- any future change that breaks a detector shows up here immediately.

What it cannot show: performance against real fraud, the false-positive rate on
genuine institutional data, or whether our thresholds are the right ones for AICTE.
Those need a pilot with real returns and human adjudication.

## How to read this

- **Precision** = of the flags we raised for a scenario, how many were real.
- **Recall** = of the cases we injected, how many we caught.
- A legal dual degree reported as fraud is the worst error this system can make, so
  it is checked separately and must stay at zero.
- The thresholds behind these numbers live in `api/app/rules/*.yaml` and in the
  detector modules; they were not tuned per scenario to flatter this table. Two
  genuine gaps found while building this harness are recorded in the git history:
  late admissions in seeded data were only flagged at institution level, and the
  seat matrix double-counted continuing supernumerary students.
